How often should a modern mechanical watch really be serviced?

H
HorologyMaster_Geneva
Senior Watchmaker • Posted 2 hours ago

For decades, Swiss manufacturers have recommended a complete overhaul service every 3 to 5 years. However, with the universal adoption of modern synthetic micro-lubricants (such as Moebius 9010 on high-speed escapement pivots and HP-1300 on high-torque train wheels), oil gumming and coagulation are virtually non-existent compared to historic animal/vegetable oils.

In our Geneva workshop, we often see calibers running after 8-10 years with acceptable delta rates, but dry pivots do eventually cause brass and steel wear (pivoting arbors grinding against dry jewels).

What is the collective consensus here: Should collectors service strictly every 5 years proactively, or wait until Timegrapher amplitude drops below 240° / beat error exceeds 0.6ms?

The primary risk of extending past 7 years is not rate loss, but automatic rotor axle wear. The oscillating weight exerts continuous radial torque on its center post. Once the lubrication evaporates, the rotor rubs against the movement bridges, generating fine metal shavings that circulate through the gear train. An annual pressure test + 5-7 year caliber service is our standard recommendation.

A
AureliaGemologist
GIA GG • 45 mins ago

Also consider gasket degradation! Even if the movement is running smoothly, rubber crown and caseback O-rings dry out after 3-4 years, compromising water resistance. You might not need a full teardown, but gasket renewal is non-negotiable for daily sports watches.
